Lesson 01 / Foundations / Viking Age

Who were
the Vikings?

The popular image begins with a warrior. History begins with a much larger world.

R3X Studio5 min readUpdated July 2026

In this lesson

A word, not one nationA wider Viking worldMyth and evidenceSource trail

“Viking” is useful, but it can also flatten a complex period into one costume. Most people living in Viking-age Scandinavia were not raiders. They built farms, raised families, made tools, traded goods and lived within local communities.

A word, not one nation

The people we now group under the Viking label came mainly from the regions that became Denmark, Norway and Sweden. They did not belong to one unified Viking state, and they did not all share the same occupation. Political power was local and changed over time as rulers, trading centres and kingdoms developed.

The Viking Age is dated differently by institutions and archaeologists. A practical frame is roughly AD 750–1100, while narrower national chronologies may use about AD 800–1050. These dates mark a historical process, not a switch that turned one culture on and off overnight.

The warrior is part of the story. He is not the whole society.

A wider Viking world

Scandinavian seafarers travelled to raid, trade, settle and serve abroad. Their routes reached the British Isles, the North Atlantic, the rivers of eastern Europe and markets connected to the Byzantine and Islamic worlds.

At home, society was largely agrarian. Farmers stood at its centre, alongside craftspeople and merchants. Status mattered: powerful landowners occupied the top of a hierarchy that also included free households and enslaved people.

Myth and evidence

Modern Viking culture often mixes archaeology, medieval literary sources and recent symbolism. That mixture can inspire art, but it should be labelled clearly. There is no evidence for the familiar horned battle helmet, and not every symbol marketed as “Viking” belongs to the Viking Age.

The Old Code keeps a simple rule: separate what the evidence supports, what later sources describe, and what modern creators interpret. Respect begins with that distinction.
