Lesson 04 / Society / Power

Jarls, Karls
and Thralls

The Viking world was connected by family and honour—and divided by wealth, power and freedom.

R3X Studio7 min readUpdated July 2026

In this lesson

Viking-age society was hierarchical. The familiar words jarl, karl and thrall offer a memorable outline, but reality differed by place and changed across three centuries.

A useful model

The Old Norse poem Rígsþula presents society through three symbolic groups: thralls, karls and jarls. Modern summaries often translate them as enslaved people, free farmers and aristocrats. The model is useful, but it is poetry—not a census or a complete legal code.

Archaeology and later law reveal many gradations inside each broad level. Wealthy farmers, land-poor free people, merchants, craftspeople, warriors and dependants did not all hold the same resources or influence.

Magnates and rulers

At the top stood kings and powerful magnates. Their authority depended on land, wealth, reputation and loyal followers. A king was not an absolute modern monarch; several claimants could compete, and royal power relied on alliances with regional elites.

Great halls were more than homes. They were places for feasting, gift-giving, political decisions, ritual and the display of power. Silver and generosity helped leaders attract supporters, while military success reinforced status.

Honour mattered—but honour operated inside an unequal society.

Free and unfree

Free farmers formed a large and important part of society. They worked land, maintained households, attended assemblies and could be called into military service. Their position varied greatly: some controlled substantial farms; others possessed far less.

Thralls were unfree. They could be captured, traded, born into slavery or punished into it. Enslaved people worked in households, fields and major building projects. The Viking economy and expansion cannot be described honestly without acknowledging this exploitation.

The limits of three labels

Gender, family connections, age, regional law and personal wealth shaped opportunity alongside formal freedom. Social movement was possible in some circumstances, but the system was not equal. The three labels are a doorway into the subject, not its final map.
